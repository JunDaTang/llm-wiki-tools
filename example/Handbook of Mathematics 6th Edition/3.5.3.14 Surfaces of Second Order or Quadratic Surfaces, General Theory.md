##### 3.5.3.14 Surfaces of Second Order or Quadratic Surfaces, General Theory

1. General Equation of a Surface of Second Order

$$
a _ { 1 1 } x ^ { 2 } + a _ { 2 2 } y ^ { 2 } + a _ { 3 3 } z ^ { 2 } + 2 a _ { 1 2 } x y + 2 a _ { 2 3 } y z + 2 a _ { 3 1 } z x + 2 a _ { 1 4 } x + 2 a _ { 2 4 } y + 2 a _ { 3 4 } z + a _ { 4 4 } = 0 . 0 2 2 a x + a _ { 4 4 } x\tag{3.428}
$$

2. Telling the Type of Second-Order Surface from its Equation

The type of a second-order surface can be determined from its equation by the signs of its invariants $\Delta , \delta , { \overset { \circ } { S } }$ , and T from Tables 3.24 and 3.25. Here one can find the names with the normal form of the equation of the surfaces, and every equation can be transformed into a normal form. From the equation of the so-called imaginary surfaces it is impossible to determine the coordinates of any real point, except the vertex of the imaginary cone, and the intersection line of two imaginary planes.
