#### 17.2.6 Chaos in One-Dimensional Mappings

For continuous mappings of a compact interval into itself, there exist several suficient conditions for the existence of chaotic invariant sets. Three examples are to be considered.

Shinai Theorem: Let $\varphi \colon I  I$ be a continuous mapping of a compact interval $I , \mathrm { e . g . } , I = [ 0 , 1 ]$ into itself. Then the system $\{ \varphi ^ { k } \}$ on I is chaotic in the sense of Devaney if and only if the topological entropy of $\varphi$ on $I , \operatorname { i . e . , } h ( \varphi )$ , is positive.

Sharkovsky Theorem: Consider the following ordering of positive integer numbers:

$$
3 \times 5 \times 7 \times \ldots \sim 2 \cdot 3 \sim 2 \cdot 5 \sim \ldots \sim 2 ^ { 2 } \cdot 3 \sim 2 ^ { 2 } \cdot 5 \sim \ldots \sim 2 ^ { 3 } \succ 2 ^ { 2 } \succ 2 \succ 1 .\tag{17.60}
$$

Let $\varphi \colon I  I$ be a continuous mapping of a compact interval into itself and suppose $\{ \varphi ^ { k } \}$ has an nperiodic orbit on I. Then $\{ \varphi ^ { k } \}$ also has an m-periodic orbit if $n \succ m$

Block, Guckenheimer and Misiuriewicz Theorem: Let $\varphi : I  I$ be a continuous mapping of the compact interval I into itself such that $\{ \varphi ^ { k } \}$ has a 2<sup>n</sup>m-periodic orbit $( m > 1 , \mathrm { o d d } )$ . Then $h ( \varphi ) \geq { \frac { \ln 2 } { 2 ^ { n + 1 } } }$ holds.
